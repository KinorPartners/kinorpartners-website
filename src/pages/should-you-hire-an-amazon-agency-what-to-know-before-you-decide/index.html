Should you hire an Amazon agency? Usually not yet.

Short answer: most sellers under about $10,000 a month on Amazon should not hire an agency yet, and most sellers over about $30,000 a month are leaving money on the table by not getting help with advertising. Between those two lines it depends on your margin, your time and your catalog. This guide gives you the numbers to decide, including what we charge and two of our own accounts with the figures attached.

The decision in one table

Agencies charge a retainer, so the question is whether the improvement they make is larger than the fee. Below a certain revenue it cannot be, however good they are.

Your monthly Amazon revenueWhat usually makes senseWhy
Under $10,000Do it yourself with free resourcesA $2,000 retainer is 20% or more of revenue. No agency can earn that back on a small base. Use Amazon's own training and our guides.
$10,000 to $30,000A freelancer or a PPC-only engagementAdvertising is where a specialist pays back fastest. Buy that one skill by the hour or on a small retainer; keep listings and operations in house.
Over $30,000A full-service agency, if the fee stays under about 10% of revenueAt this size a 5-point improvement in ACoS or conversion is worth more than the fee, and your time is worth more than the daily work.
Any size, margin under 25%Fix the margin firstThin margins leave no room for a fee and no room for the ad spend an agency will ask you to run. Reprice, renegotiate or change the product before you hire.

A rule of thumb: keep total outside help, fee plus tools, under 10% of Amazon revenue. If the only way to afford an agency is to stop advertising, you are not ready for one.

When not to hire an agency

These are the cases where we tell people not to hire us. Each one is a problem an agency cannot fix from the outside.

  • You have no reviews yet. Ads and listing work cannot overcome a product with zero social proof. Get your first 15 to 25 reviews through Amazon Vine and the Request a Review button, then talk to an agency.
  • Your margin is under 25% after Amazon fees. There is no room for a retainer or for the ad spend that growth needs. See the FBA versus FBM guide; the fulfillment choice is often where the margin went.
  • You are not in Brand Registry. Without it there is no A+ Content, no Sponsored Brands and no Brand Analytics, which removes most of what an agency would work with. Enroll first; it needs only a pending trademark.
  • You sell one SKU. A single product needs a few hours of setup and a weekly check on its ads, not a retainer. A freelancer or a few hours of consulting is the right size.
  • You want a guarantee. Nobody can guarantee rank or sales on Amazon. An agency that promises a number is either guessing or planning to buy it with your ad budget.
  • You cannot give access. An agency needs Seller Central user permissions and your ad console. If that is not possible, consulting is the only honest option.

What to ask before you sign with anyone

  • Show me a case study at my revenue size, with the fee included in the result.
  • Who does the daily work on my account, and how many accounts do they carry?
  • What do I get every week, and do I keep access to my own ad data?
  • Do you work with my direct competitors?
  • What is the contract length, the notice period and the exit terms?
  • Which tools are included in the fee, and which do I pay for?

Red flags

  • A guaranteed sales figure or rank.
  • "It depends" pricing with no written scope.
  • A 12-month lock-in with no performance clause.
  • No access to your own advertising console.
  • Any mention of buying reviews, "rank juice" or rebate clubs.
  • A case study with a percentage and no base figure.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an Amazon agency typically cost?

Most full-service Amazon agencies charge $1,500 to $5,000 a month for small to mid-size sellers; the larger firms start at $3,500 and require $20,000 to $30,000 a month in revenue. Performance-based models take 8 to 15% of ad spend or revenue. Kinor Partners starts at $2,000 a month with a revenue-share option for qualifying brands.

How long before an agency shows results?

Advertising efficiency usually moves inside 60 to 90 days, because campaign structure and bids are under direct control. Organic rank from listing and SEO work takes 3 to 6 months. Anyone promising faster is describing ad spend, not growth.

Can a small business afford an Amazon agency?

Above roughly $30,000 a month on Amazon, usually yes: a few points of ACoS or conversion are worth more than the fee. Below $10,000 a month, usually no, and the honest answer is to use free training and a freelancer for the one skill you lack. Between the two, buy PPC management only.

What is the difference between a full-service agency and a PPC-only agency?

A PPC-only agency manages your advertising. A full-service agency manages ads, listing optimization, A+ Content, Brand Registry, reviews and inventory planning together. PPC-only is cheaper and is the right first purchase for most sellers under $30,000 a month.

Agency or freelancer?

A freelancer is the right choice for one job: a listing rewrite, a PPC audit, photography. An agency is the right choice when you need the jobs coordinated and someone accountable for the whole account every week. Price the freelancer by the hour and the agency by the result minus the fee.
