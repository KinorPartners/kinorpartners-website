Seller tools, by job
What each category is for and the tools sellers most often use. No affiliate links; we are not paid by any of them

Product and keyword research

Helium 10 and Jungle Scout are the two suites most sellers pick between; both estimate demand, track keywords and audit listings. DataDive goes deeper on keyword ranking for an established catalog. You need one of these, not all three.

Price and sales history

Keepa and camelcamelcamel chart an ASIN's price, Buy Box and rank history. Both have free tiers and are the fastest way to check a competitor's real behavior before you launch against them.

Advertising management

Start in the Amazon Ads console itself; it is free and enough for most small catalogs. Pacvue and Perpetua add bid automation and are worth it once spend passes a few thousand dollars a month. Our PPC guide below covers campaign structure first, because no tool fixes a bad structure.

Profit and finance

Sellerboard turns Seller Central reports into a daily profit view after fees, ads and returns. If you only add one paid tool, make it the one that tells you whether you are actually making money.

Inventory forecasting

SoStocked and Inventory Planner forecast reorders and FBA shipments. Below roughly ten SKUs a spreadsheet and our inventory guide are enough.

Reviews and feedback

FeedbackWhiz automates the compliant "Request a Review" button and alerts you to new reviews. Anything that promises reviews rather than requests is a policy risk; see our reviews guide.

Frequently asked questions
Quick answers to the questions Amazon sellers ask us most
What is the most important factor for ranking on Amazon?
Sales velocity and conversion rate are the two biggest drivers of Amazon rankings. Amazon's A9 algorithm prioritizes products that generate the most revenue per customer search. That means your listing needs to convert well (strong images, compelling title, clear bullet points) and you need consistent sales momentum, ideally supported by PPC advertising in the early stages. Keyword relevance is the foundation, but conversion and sales velocity are what sustain rankings over time.
How long does it take to see results on Amazon?
Most new sellers see meaningful traction within 60 to 90 days when following a structured launch strategy. Initial keyword rankings typically improve within 2 to 4 weeks if you're running targeted PPC and your listing is properly optimized. Review accumulation usually begins in weeks 3 to 6. Sustainable organic ranking and profitability typically emerge in months 3 to 6.
Should I use FBA or FBM for my products?
FBA (Fulfilled by Amazon) is the right choice for most small businesses selling products under 10 lbs with healthy margins, because it gives you Prime eligibility and handles all logistics. FBM (Fulfilled by Merchant) makes more sense for oversized, heavy, or fragile items where FBA fees would destroy margins. Many successful sellers use a hybrid approach: FBA for fast-moving SKUs and FBM as a backup.
How much should I spend on Amazon PPC?
A common starting benchmark is spending 10 to 15% of your target monthly revenue on PPC during the launch phase, then scaling back as organic rankings improve. Focus on ACOS (Advertising Cost of Sale); most sellers target 20 to 35% ACOS depending on their margins. The key is reinvesting in profitable campaigns and cutting spend on underperformers.
Is Amazon Brand Registry worth it for small businesses?
Yes. Brand Registry is one of the highest-leverage moves a small business can make on Amazon. It unlocks A+ Content (which can increase conversion rates by 3 to 10%), Sponsored Brand ads, the Brand Store, and advanced anti-counterfeiting tools. The only requirement is a registered or pending trademark.
Which of these resources are free?
Everything Amazon publishes above is free, including Seller University and the Ads learning console. Keepa and camelcamelcamel have free tiers. Every Kinor Partners guide on this page is free with no sign-up. The research, advertising, profit and inventory tools are paid; most offer a trial.
